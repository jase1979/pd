## Sectional forms and standard sizes of natural timber

<div class="key-term" markdown="1">
**Stock form** — the standard shape and size in which a material is supplied before it is made into a product, e.g. sheets, planks, rods and granules.
</div>

Buying materials in **standard sizes** keeps them cheaper and easier to obtain, and lets designers plan parts to fit with little waste.

Natural timber is cut at the sawmill into standard **sectional forms** (the shape of the cross-section):

| Form | Description | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Plank** | A wide, thick piece with a rectangular cross-section | Workbenches, table tops, heavy shelving, construction |
| **Board** | Wide and relatively thin, with a rectangular cross-section | Shelves, cladding, box sides, furniture panels |
| **Strip** | Narrow and thin | Frames, edging, beading, light structures |
| **Square** | Equal width and thickness | Legs, frames, posts, turning blanks |
| **Dowel** | A long, round section, in diameters typically from about 6 mm to 25 mm | Dowel joints, rails, pegs, handles, axles |

Sizes are given as **width × thickness**, plus a length — for example 95 × 22 mm. Softwood is commonly sold in **standard lengths** that rise in steps of 300 mm (for example 1.8 m, 2.1 m, 2.4 m, 2.7 m and 3.0 m).

## Sawn and planed timber

| | Sawn timber | Planed timber |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Rough, straight from the saw, with visible saw marks | Smooth on all faces |
| Size | Close to its **nominal** (stated) size | Smaller than the nominal sawn size, because planing removes a few millimetres from each face |
| Cost | Cheaper | More expensive, because of the extra processing |
| Uses | Hidden structural work: roof timbers, fencing, garden structures, studwork | Furniture, joinery, visible woodwork and products made in school |

<div class="key-term" markdown="1">
**PAR (planed all round)** — timber planed smooth on all four faces. **PSE (planed square edge)** is planed on its edges and faces so the edges are square and ready to use.
</div>

<div class="exam-tip" markdown="1">
If you buy "50 × 25 mm" planed timber, it will measure a few millimetres less than that. Always check the **actual** size when designing joints and working out quantities.
</div>

## Manufactured boards: sheets, standard sizes and thicknesses

Manufactured boards are supplied as flat **sheets**. The full standard sheet size is **2440 × 1220 mm** (8 ft × 4 ft), and suppliers also sell **half sheets** (1220 × 610 mm) and smaller cut pieces.

| Board | Common thicknesses (mm) |
|---|---|
| MDF | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 25 |
| Plywood | 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 |
| Chipboard | 12, 15, 18, 22, 25 |
| Hardboard | 3.2 |

Boards are also available **pre-finished** — veneered, melamine-faced or primed — and in special grades such as **moisture-resistant MDF**, **exterior or marine plywood** and **flexible plywood**. School laser cutters typically use **3 mm** MDF and plywood cut down to fit the machine bed.

**Planning parts on a sheet:** designers lay out parts to fit the standard sheet with as little waste as possible (**nesting**), allowing for the width of each saw cut (the **kerf**).

## Standardised fixtures and fittings, including knock-down fittings

<div class="key-term" markdown="1">
**Standard component** — a part made in large quantities to an agreed size and specification, so it can be bought ready-made rather than made for each product, e.g. screws, hinges and bolts.
</div>

**Why use standard components?**

- **Cheaper** — mass production keeps the price low.
- **Reliable and consistent** — made to standards (such as BSI and ISO sizes) and easy to replace.
- **Saves time** — the designer does not have to design or make them.

**Common standardised fixtures and fittings:**

- **Screws** — wood, self-tapping and machine screws, with countersunk, round or pan heads, sized by length and gauge.
- **Nails and pins** — round wire nails, oval nails, panel pins.
- **Nuts, bolts and washers** — metric sizes such as M4, M5, M6 and M8.
- **Hinges** — butt hinges, piano (continuous) hinges, concealed kitchen-cabinet hinges, flush hinges.
- **Catches, handles and knobs** — magnetic and ball catches, pull handles.
- **Brackets and corner plates.**

<div class="key-term" markdown="1">
**Knock-down fitting (KDF)** — a fixing that lets a product be assembled, and taken apart again, using only simple tools. KDFs are the basis of **flat-pack furniture**.
</div>

| KDF | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Cam lock** | A metal dowel screwed into one panel locks into a round cam in the other panel when the cam is turned |
| **Corner (modesty) block** | A plastic block screwed into both panels to hold a corner at 90° |
| **Scan fitting** (cross dowel and bolt) | A bolt passes through one part and screws into a barrel nut set across the other |
| **Threaded insert** | A metal insert with an internal thread is fitted into the wood so a bolt can be screwed in and out repeatedly |

**Advantages of KDFs:** furniture can be **flat-packed**, which saves space in transport and storage and lowers the cost; it can be assembled by the user and taken apart to move or repair. **Disadvantages:** joints are usually weaker than glued joints and can work loose; assembly instructions can be hard to follow.

## The forms polymers are supplied in: powders and granules

Raw polymers leave the chemical plant as **granules** (also called pellets or nibs) or **powders**, so they can be measured, coloured and fed into machines easily.

| Form | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Granules / pellets** | Fed from a hopper into **injection moulding**, **blow moulding** and **extrusion** machines, where they are melted. Colour can be added as **masterbatch** granules |
| **Powders** | **Rotational moulding** (hollow products such as kayaks and road cones), **compression moulding** of thermosets, and **powder coating** and **dip coating** of metal |

Polymers are also supplied in **semi-finished** forms for schools and small-scale makers:

- **Sheet** — e.g. 3 mm acrylic for laser cutting; HIPS sheet for vacuum forming
- **Rod and tube** — for turning, handles and frames
- **Film** — for packaging and laminating
- **Filament** — on spools, such as PLA and ABS for 3D printing
- **Foam** — such as Styrofoam blocks for modelling
