## How the NEA works

- **50% of your GCSE** — the Non-Exam Assessment (NEA) is a design and make task, worth **100 marks**
- **About 35 hours** of work, starting from a contextual challenge set by WJEC
- WJEC sets **three contexts** each year. They are only starting points: they are open to interpretation, and your design journey is personal to you
- **Marked by your teacher** and then checked by an external **moderator** from WJEC
- Informal work (research, sketching, modelling) can be done in school or at home
- **Your final prototype must be made under supervised conditions in school**

---

## Sketchbook and folio

There is **no set format** for the NEA — how you present it is up to you. The recommended approach is to split your work into three parts:

| Part | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Informal sketchbook** | Exploring the contexts, research, initial ideas, sketches, photos of cardboard models, development, testing and ongoing evaluation. It can be rough, and it should show your thinking |
| **Formal presentation folio** | Final design brief and specification; pictorial and technical drawings of the final prototype; production details and sequence of production; evaluation; modifications and further developments; photographs of the final prototype |
| **Practical outcomes** | Your final prototype, plus any supporting pieces: models, jigs, formers, patterns, test pieces, trials and iterations. Keep them, or photograph them |

The page numbers used in this guide (Page 1, Page 2, …) are a **suggested order**, not a requirement. Each section says where its evidence usually goes.

---

## Design is iterative

The NEA is **not a linear process**. You should keep going round a cycle: **design → model → test → reflect**, then improve the design and repeat.

- Start sketching and modelling **from the very beginning**. Early ideas help you work out what else you need to research
- Have **several starting points** and avoid fixating on one idea
- Many ideas will be rejected. That is normal, and it is evidence of good designing
- Record what you learn from every model and test in your sketchbook

> WJEC describes this as the **interaction of mind and hand**. Thinking inside your head (imagining, speculating) only goes so far. Sketching, modelling and testing *outside* your head confront your ideas with reality, and that is what moves a design forward.

## How the marks are awarded

| Section | Assessment criteria | AO | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Identifying design possibilities | AO1 | 10 |
| **B** | Developing a design brief and specification | AO1 | 10 |
| **C** | Generating and developing design ideas | AO2 | 30 |
| **D** | Making a prototype | AO2 | 30 |
| **E** | Evaluating a prototype's fitness for purpose | AO2 | 20 |

Each section is marked using **four bands**:

- Your teacher decides which band **best fits** your work as a whole
- A mark at the **top of a band** is only given when **all** of that band's descriptors are met
- If some evidence is missing, you get a **lower mark within the band**

This is why every section of this guide ends with a **Band 4 checklist**. Tick off every item and you give yourself the best chance of reaching the top of the band.
