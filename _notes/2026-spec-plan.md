# Plan: WJEC GCSE Design & Technology (2026 spec) section

Source: `WJEC GCSE Design and Technology Specification` — teaching from 2026, first award 2028,
Qualification Approval Number C00/5167/8. © WJEC CBAC Ltd 2025.

This document is the build plan for adding the new specification as a second top-level section
alongside the existing (legacy) GCSE Product Design material. It is stored in `_notes/`, which
Jekyll does not publish.

---

## 1. What the new spec actually is

It is **not** a revision of the current site's spec — it is a different qualification with a
different shape, so it needs its own top-level section rather than edits to the existing pages.

| | Legacy (current site) | New (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Title | WJEC GCSE Design and Technology (Product Design) | WJEC GCSE Design and Technology ("Made for Wales") |
| Unit 1 | Written exam, `3603U10-1` | **Digital/on-screen exam**, 1h30, **80 marks, 30%** |
| Unit 2 | NEA, sections A–E (10/10/30/30/20 = 80) | **Design Project NEA, 100 marks, 70%**, ~40 hours |
| Structure | Core + Product Design in-depth | Core + **one of three pathways** |
| Pathways | — | Engineering Design / Fashion & Textiles / **Product Design** |
| Availability | — | Wales only; not available to centres in England |
| First award | current | **2028** |

Key rules worth surfacing to students on the site:

- Learners pick **one pathway** and must use the **same pathway in Unit 1 and Unit 2**.
- Unit 2 contextual challenges are released by WJEC on **1 April** via Portal; the unit can be
  completed any time after release but is submitted in the final year. Unit 1 is examined in the
  final year. The qualification is **linear** and **not tiered**.
- Unit 2 is centre-assessed, externally moderated by WJEC.
- Assessment objective weightings:

  | | AO1 | AO2 | AO3 | AO4 | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Unit 1 | 15% | 15% | — | — | 30% |
  | Unit 2 | — | 15% | 35% | 20% | 70% |
  | Overall | 15% | 30% | 35% | 20% | 100% |

  AO1 knowledge & understanding · AO2 apply it · AO3 develop designs and tangible products ·
  AO4 analyse and evaluate products and solutions.

### Amplification stems (drives how each page should be written)

The spec's right-hand column uses five stems, and they set the required depth. Page content
should mirror them rather than flattening everything to "know this":

- *Learners should know* — basic knowledge and understanding.
- *Learners should be able to* — apply to a practical situation / demonstrate a skill.
- *Learners should understand* — greater depth, application to unfamiliar contexts, analysis and evaluation.
- *Learners should be aware of* — does not need detail.
- *Learners are required to* — a requirement of the unit (mostly Unit 2).

Also: "**including**" = must be taught and can be assessed. "**for example**"/"**such as**" = guidance
only, alternatives allowed. Worth encoding as a visual distinction on topic pages — an
"assessable" vs "example only" marker — because it tells students what is actually examinable.

### Existing files that must change

1. **`_data/nav.yml`** — four new unit records with `level: dt2026`. Also worth relabelling the
   legacy units, because the two specs' numbering collides head-on: legacy "2.1.1 Core Knowledge"
   vs new "2.1.1 Contextual challenges and user-centred design". Without a spec label in the
   breadcrumb these are indistinguishable in search results and bookmarks.

2. **`_layouts/topic.html`** — two problems here, one of them pre-existing.

   a. *Existing bug, currently live.* Line 73 reads
      `{% assign _nav_prev = _flat[_curr_idx | minus: 1] %}`. Liquid cannot apply a filter inside
      an array subscript, so this is a syntax error — the build prints
      `Liquid Warning: Expected close_square but found pipe` once per topic page, and every topic
      page except the first in its unit renders an empty previous link: `<a href="">&larr; </a>`.
      A clickable arrow that goes nowhere, on 21 pages. The "next" links are fine. Fix is one
      line — compute the index first:

      ```liquid
      {% assign _prev_idx = _curr_idx | minus: 1 %}
      {% assign _nav_prev = _flat[_prev_idx] %}
      ```

   b. *Blocker for this project.* The prev/next chain flattens **all** of `site.data.nav` into a
      single sequence. As soon as the new units are added, the last legacy page links forward
      into the new spec. Fix: filter the flattened list by the current page's level before
      computing prev/next.

   Both are prerequisites for Phase 1, not polish items.

3. **`_includes/header.html`** — add the new section; disambiguate the existing "GCSE" link
   (e.g. "GCSE (legacy)" / "D&T 2026").

4. **`_sass/_variables.scss`** — add `--dt2026` accent colour (violet `#a78bfa` reads well
   against the existing teal `--gcse` and orange `--accent`). Then follow the existing
   per-level pattern in `_header.scss`, `_home.scss`, `_glossary.scss`, `_past-papers.scss`.

5. **`glossary/index.html`** — badge class is built as `glossary-badge--{{ entry.level | downcase }}`.
   A level value containing a space or `&` produces a broken class name, so this needs
   `| slugify` before any new-spec terms are added. Also add a spec filter, since the glossary
   will hold terms from two specifications.

6. **`index.html` / `_includes/level-units.html`** — already loop over `site.data.levels` and
   filter by level, so they pick up the new section for free once the data is in place. Worth
   adding a "new spec / legacy" cue to the home cards.

7. **`past-papers/index.html`** — the header says "WJEC GCSE Design and Technology – Product
   Design (Unit 1: 3603U10-1)", which is the legacy paper. Label it explicitly as legacy so
   students on the new spec aren't misled about which questions apply.

## 5. Resources to go with the section

0. **Drag-and-drop activities** — the `drag-puzzle` component ported from the comp-sci site
   (`_includes/drag-puzzle.html`, `_sass/_puzzles.scss`, definitions in `_data/puzzles.yml`).
   Two types: `order` for sequencing and `match` for term-to-definition. Phase 2 added ten
   across the Unit 1 core pages, and **every content page from Phase 3 onwards gets at least
   one**, placed so it is answerable from content on the same page.

   Two things to keep in mind when authoring them. Terms become chips and do not wrap, so keep
   them short. And do not build an `order` puzzle for a process the page describes as cyclic —
   the commercial development process is a `match` for exactly that reason.

   Known limitation, inherited from the source site: the shuffle runs in JavaScript on load, so
   if the SortableJS CDN fails the items render in their correct order. Shuffling server-side in
   Liquid would remove both the CDN dependency and the giveaway.

## 5a. Scope: unit knowledge and skills only

Confirmed scope is the **assessed unit content** — 1.1.x and 2.1.x knowledge and skills — and
nothing else from the specification. Deliberately **out of scope**:

- Section 1 in full: aims, Curriculum for Wales, prior learning and progression, guided learning
  hours, use of language, equality and fair access.
- "Opportunities for integration of learning experiences" (p. 42). The specification states these
  **will not be directly assessed**. This is also the only place careers appear, so there is no
  assessed careers content to build resources for.
- Sections 4 and 5: malpractice, making entries, grading and awarding.
- Appendix A: mapping to Curriculum for Wales cross-curricular themes and integral skills.

Note that **"pathway" is an assessment route, not a careers route**. It determines which in-depth
Unit 1 content a learner studies and which specialist skills Unit 2 assesses, and the only rule
attached to it is that the same pathway must be used in both units. It does not commit a learner
to a career, and progression to AS/A level is open whichever pathway was taken.

The assessment overview page at `/gcse-2026/assessment/` is trimmed to what supports revision:
unit format, marks and weightings, the assessment objectives, the amplification stems and the
"including" vs "for example" distinction, and the note that the Unit 2 mark breakdown is
unpublished. Regulatory and administrative material (Qualifications Wales, availability, linear
and untiered, entry timing) has been removed.

## 6. Open questions and things not to invent

- **Unit 2 mark breakdown is not in this specification.** It gives 100 marks, 70%, ~40 hours and
  the AO weightings, and states that the Assessment Pack "will include all detailed information
  relating to assessment". The legacy A–E split (10/10/30/30/20) must **not** be carried over or
  implied. The Unit 2 pages should present the six content areas and say plainly that the mark
  allocation is pending the Assessment Pack.
- **The Unit 1 exam is digital/on-screen** and asks for "objective responses, short and extended
  answers". Practice material should reflect objective-response formats, which the legacy written
  papers do not contain.
- **Guidance for Teaching** is referenced repeatedly for depth and breadth, particularly on
  "learners should be aware of" content. It is a separate document and is not in this PDF.
- **Two decisions, both now settled:**
  1. *Pathway coverage* — **Product Design only**. The URL and data structure
     (`unit1/product-design/`) leaves room to add Engineering Design and Fashion & Textiles later
     without restructuring. The core content (1.1.1–1.1.5, 2.1.1–2.1.6) is shared by all three
     pathways, so it is written once regardless.
  2. *Legacy section's future* — **keep both, clearly labelled**. Legacy cohorts are still sitting
     exams while the new spec is being taught.
